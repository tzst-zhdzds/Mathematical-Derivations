Properties of Relations and Equivalence Relations

A binary relation from A to B is a subset of A × B. When both sets are A, we call R a relation on A. In that case, R ⊆ A × A.

Reflexivity

A relation R on A is reflexive if, for every element a in A, the ordered pair (a, a) belongs to R.

For every a ∈ A, a R a.

Symmetry

A relation R on A is symmetric if, whenever (a, b) belongs to R, the reversed pair (b, a) also belongs to R.

For all a, b ∈ A, if a R b, then b R a.

Transitivity

A relation R on A is transitive if, whenever (a, b) and (b, c) both belong to R, the pair (a, c) also belongs to R.

For all a, b, c ∈ A, if a R b and b R c, then a R c.

Equivalence Relations

A relation R on A is an equivalence relation if and only if it is reflexive, symmetric, and transitive.

Example

On the set of integers, let a R b mean a ≤ b.

  • Reflexive? Yes. Every integer a satisfies a ≤ a.
  • Symmetric? No. We have 1 ≤ 2, but 2 ≤ 1 is false.
  • Transitive? Yes. If a ≤ b and b ≤ c, then a ≤ c.

The pair (1, 2) gives a counterexample to symmetry. Therefore, ≤ is not an equivalence relation on the integers.

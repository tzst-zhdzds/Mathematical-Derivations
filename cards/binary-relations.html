Binary Relations

Let A and B be sets. A binary relation R from A to B is any subset of the Cartesian product A × B. We write:

R ⊆ A × B

To form a relation, we choose which ordered pairs from A × B to include. We can choose no pairs at all, in which case R = ∅, or we can choose every pair, in which case R = A × B.

Ex: Let A = {1, 2} and B = {3, 4}. Their Cartesian product is:

A × B = {(1, 3), (1, 4), (2, 3), (2, 4)}

Define R by choosing the pairs (x, y) in which the second component is exactly 2 more than the first.

When x = 1, this gives y = 3. When x = 2, it gives y = 4. So the relation is:

R = {(1, 3), (2, 4)}

Relation Notation

We write a R b to mean that (a, b) belongs to R. We read this as "a is related to b by R."

a R b ⇔ (a, b) ∈ R

In the example above, 1 R 3 is true because (1, 3) ∈ R. The statement 1 R 4 is false. Although (1, 4) belongs to A × B, it does not belong to R because 4 ≠ 1 + 2.
